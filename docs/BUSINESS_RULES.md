# Business rules and detection

1. Stock at time T = opening units + sum of signed movement quantities with timestamps <= T. Receipts, sellable returns and transfer-in increase stock; sales, damage and transfer-out reduce stock. Adjustments may have either sign. Every outgoing baseline event must have sufficient stock; replenishment is an explicit receipt.
2. A transfer has two reciprocal movement references, the same transfer ID and SKU, different stores, equal quantities and a net signed quantity of zero. The deliberate double-debit exception records a negative transfer-in, so both stores lose stock. The ledger retains this faulty event; reconciliation detects and reports it.
3. A movement's shift must belong to the same store and contain its timestamp. Wrong-store injections deliberately reference another store's shift, without breaking master-data foreign keys.
4. STORE_OPERATOR may sell, receive, return, record damage and transfer. MANAGER may also adjust. WAREHOUSE_OPERATOR may receive, return, record damage and transfer. ANALYST may not post movements. All count workers and investigation owners use valid roles; investigation owners must be managers.
5. The detector flags missing adjustment reasons; three negative adjustments for the same store/SKU in a rolling 60-second window (all involved records); store/shift mismatch; shift-time violations; unauthorized movement types; broken transfer pairs; count reference timestamps after the count; and negative count variance of at least five units.
6. Count reference movement must have the same store/SKU. Expected stock is derived as of the count timestamp even for the planted reference-order exception. Counts are observations and do not silently update stock.
7. Every return links to an earlier sale for the same store/SKU. Total returned units cannot exceed units sold in that sale. Generated returns are SELLABLE; legitimate non-sellable loss is represented by DAMAGE movements with DAMAGED_GOODS reasons. Neither damage nor return alone triggers an alert.
8. Exposure is absolute affected quantity times SKU unit cost in INR. HIGH exposure is >= INR 1,000 (score 0.9); other alerts are MEDIUM (score 0.6). Scores are ordinal rule tiers, not probabilities.
9. Operational CSVs have no planted labels. Ground truth records injected scenarios separately. Detection never reads ground truth. Evaluation reads it only after predictions exist.
10. Investigation status follows OPEN -> IN_PROGRESS -> CLOSED. Every state must have an append-only InvestigationEvent; the final state/timestamp must match the snapshot. Closing requires an outcome. Generation initializes OPEN investigations, and regression tests exercise both transitions.
11. Validation permits only exact record/rule structural exceptions declared in ground truth. It fails on unexpected violations and on expected structural exceptions that are absent. Planted shortage and adjustment patterns are evaluated as business alerts rather than arithmetic corruption.
12. Synthetic evaluation treats all unplanted records as negatives and reports overall record-level metrics, per-rule metrics and explicit benign results. Real-world accuracy must be evaluated separately.
